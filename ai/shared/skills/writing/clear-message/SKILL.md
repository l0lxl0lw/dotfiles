# Clear Message

Make the reader's job easy: notice the point, understand it, trust it, and know what to do next.

## Procedure

1. Identify the reader, channel, main point, and desired outcome. Infer these from the input; ask only when a missing detail would materially change the rewrite.
2. Check every concept below. Apply relevant improvements; do not force a story, sales hook, or request into a message that needs none.
3. Explain the meaningful changes, then deliver one ready-to-use rewrite. Preserve the writer's intent, facts, voice, and necessary nuance. Keep strong wording that already works.

## Concepts

### 1. Audience and empathy
Write to one specific reader. Match their knowledge, concerns, relationship, and channel norms. Supply context they lack. For group messages, identify who needs to pay attention.

### 2. Benefits and relevance
Answer “Why should this reader care?” Ask “So what?” about each feature or fact until its practical value is clear. Lead with a supported benefit or consequence; retain technical features when they help the reader decide. Do not invent emotional motivations.

### 3. Focus
Choose one main takeaway and, when needed, one primary request. Keep supporting ideas connected to it. Test an “and” list by asking whether the parts connect through “so” or “because”; split unrelated topics. This is a logic check, not a mechanical word replacement.

### 4. Opening and attention
Make the subject, headline, and opening specific. Put the main point near the top. Use a relevant benefit, concrete detail, or useful contrast to stand out. Keep a brief greeting when the relationship calls for it. Avoid vague teasers, clickbait, and artificial urgency.

### 5. Plain language
Prefer familiar words, direct verbs, and straightforward sentences. Split tangled clauses. Define necessary jargon for this audience. Replace abstract corporate phrasing with who does what. Keep precision and natural sentence rhythm.

### 6. Structure and scanning
Order information by importance. Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and bullets where they help. Emphasize only key information; excessive bolding hides priorities. Use concise, descriptive link labels. Move optional detail below the core message without hiding essential conditions.

### 7. Concrete support
Replace vague claims with relevant evidence, a specific example, or a short story. Use a comparison, table, or diagram only if it makes understanding easier. Never invent statistics, testimonials, credentials, or results; flag missing proof or use a labeled placeholder.

### 8. Credibility and tone
Show relevant competence through evidence rather than boasting. Be warm, respectful, and attentive to the reader's interests. Use calm, clear direction when the situation needs it. Admit genuine uncertainty or limitations; do not polish them into false certainty or manufacture vulnerability.

### 9. Minimum sufficient detail
Remove repetition, filler, side topics, and unnecessary requests. After each cut, check whether meaning, warmth, evidence, or action becomes harder to understand. Restore what is needed. Optimize reader effort, not the lowest word count.

### 10. Easy action and feedback
State the next step explicitly: what, who, and when, if known. Put necessary links and decision information beside the request. Offer a simple response format when appropriate; use a focused question when genuine discussion is needed. For an update, say “No action needed” when true.

### 11. Reader check
Skim only the opening, headings, and emphasized text: are the point, relevance, and next step clear? Recheck the rewrite against the original facts. For important campaigns, suggest testing with a representative reader; do not claim testing occurred. For follow-ups, preserve the core message and respect recipient preferences rather than prescribing a universal sending frequency.

## Channel adjustments

- **Slack:** Point or ask first, essential context next, clear reply or action.
- **Email:** Specific subject, purpose, supporting detail, explicit next step.
- **Blog:** Clear promise, early takeaway, useful sections and examples; preserve worthwhile depth.
- **Promotional email:** Reader benefit, credible support, offer and material terms, one primary call to action. Use only supplied prices, deadlines, and claims.

## Output

### What to update and why
Use concise bullets: **Concept — specific change → reader benefit.** Cover material issues without padding the review to mention every concept. If the draft already works, say so.

### Revised version
Provide one complete, paste-ready draft, including a subject or headline when appropriate. Keep commentary outside it. Briefly flag any assumptions or placeholders that need confirmation.
