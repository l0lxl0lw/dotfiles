# Sources and coverage

Reviewed October 1, 2026. Principles are paraphrased and combined; channel templates and review format are practical adaptations, not quoted frameworks.

## Todd Rogers — The 6 Principles for Writing Messages People Won't Swipe Away

[Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dJG5SMOdvEU). English automatic captions downloaded with `yt-dlp`; caption text can contain transcription errors.

- 10:24–13:01: Readers skim and decide whether, when, and how deeply to engage, then whether to respond. Concepts 1, 4, 6, 10, 11.
- 13:23–18:19: Less is more: fewer words, ideas, and requests. Concepts 3, 9.
- 18:20–21:49: Make reading easy. Concept 5.
- 21:52–28:53: Design for navigation; main point first, headings, bullets, visuals, and sensitivity to relationship norms. Concepts 4, 6, 7.
- 28:56–32:33: Use enough formatting, but no more. Concept 6.
- 32:35–36:09: Explain why readers should care and which readers are affected. Concepts 1, 2, 4.
- 36:11–39:40: Make responding easy; provide the information needed to act. Concept 10.
- 40:02–44:56: Balance reminders with fatigue and recipient preferences. Concept 11.

## Ben Guttmann — The 5 Factors for Crafting Simple Messages

[AoM #971 transcript](https://www.artofmanliness.com/finance/career/podcast-971-the-5-factors-for-crafting-simple-read-effective-messages/).

Beneficial, focused, salient, empathetic, and minimal map to concepts 2, 3, 4, 1, and 9. Includes the “So what?” benefit test, “and” versus “so” focus test, subtractive editing, and testing with representative readers. Minimal means least friction, not necessarily shortest. These are design principles, not requirements to force into every message.

## Joseph McCormack — The Power of Brevity in a Noisy World

[AoM #666 transcript / How to Be a Better Communicator](https://www.artofmanliness.com/character/self-improvement/how-to-be-a-better-communicator/).

Clarity before concision; map the main idea and support; illustrate with stories or visuals; talk, actively listen, and converse. Concepts 3, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11. Specific email subjects and explicit awareness/feedback/decision requests inform the email template. Written feedback questions adapt the conversational listening principle.

## Steve Martin — Why People Do (Or Don't) Listen to You

[AoM #580 transcript](https://www.artofmanliness.com/social/social-skills/podcast-580-why-people-do-or-dont-listen-to-you/).

The messenger influences reception. Status-oriented “hard” traits and connection-oriented “soft” traits work differently by context. Relevant expertise, credible introductions, warmth, honest limitations, shared goals, and useful metaphors inform concepts 7 and 8. The skill adapts these into evidence and tone; it does not turn physical attractiveness, wealth, or dominance cues into writing requirements.
