# hormozi-office-hours

The **diagnostic** room. You don't know what's wrong; this figures it out, then
either fixes it or walks you to the right sibling skill.

Distilled from the office-hours Q&A sessions on Alex Hormozi's MoreMozi channel —
the format where a business owner states their numbers and gets a blunt
five-minute diagnosis in front of a room.

## Where it sits

The three `100m/` skills come from the books and each owns one failure. This one
comes from the live sessions and owns the question *"which of those is my
problem?"* — plus everything the books don't cover.

```
don't know what's wrong                      -> /hormozi-office-hours
not enough people see it                     -> /100m-leads
they see it and don't buy                    -> /100m-offers
they buy and there's no cash                 -> /100m-money
can't deliver / talent / you're the product   -> /hormozi-office-hours
too many offers, locations, or businesses     -> /hormozi-office-hours
sell it, kill it, or keep it                  -> /hormozi-office-hours
```

The books are deeper on acquisition, offer, and money models, and this skill hands
off rather than duplicating them. What they don't cover is most of what the
sessions are actually about: delivery capacity, thin margins at full utilization,
talent that leaves with your customers, founders who are the product,
over-expansion, and what to kill.

For pre-revenue "is this worth building at all", use gstack `/office-hours`
instead — that's demand discovery, not constraint diagnosis.

## What a session looks like

```
Phase 0  the four-line intake, and it won't proceed without your numbers
Phase 1  challenge the premise — your question is usually the wrong one
Phase 2  find the one rate limiter: 20x the goal, see what breaks, then bisect
Phase 3  verify with margin / close rate / churn; separate luck from skill
Phase 4  one ordered prescription, plus an explicit not-yet list
Phase 5  one assignment you could start this week
Phase 6  optional one-pager, so a later session can check if you did it
```

The intake is the load-bearing part. Every guest on the channel opens the same way,
and the skill enforces it:

> I sell [what] to [whom]. We do $[revenue] at [margin]%. I'd love to be at
> $[goal] by [when]. What's stopping me is [your guess].

That last line is treated as a hypothesis to be tested, never as the brief — being
wrong about it is the most common reason people are stuck.

## Notes on the source material

These are distillations of method for working through your own business, not
summaries or transcripts. Thresholds that come from him — speed to lead in
minutes, a rough net margin floor, the six-month expansion test — are carried as
strong defaults with the reasoning attached, and the skill says out loud that
they're heuristics rather than laws.

`verticals.md` includes a deliberate **where he's wrong** section. His calibration
set is owner-operated businesses with real marginal cost — home services, clinics,
gyms, trades, agencies, coaching. Several prescriptions fail for software, most
sharply "spend more on paid acquisition" for developer tools. The diagnostic method
transfers; a chunk of the prescriptions need translating, and the skill is told to
say which is which.

The skill is instructed never to invent your numbers, benchmarks, or case studies.
A number you don't know gets named as a gap, which is often the most useful output
of the session.
