# Step 1: Engaged leads, and the six-step lead magnet

## First: count the right thing

**A lead is a contact. An engaged lead is a contact who has shown interest.** Only the
second one is worth anything, and conflating them is how people convince themselves their
marketing is working.

Ask: *"How many people last week did something that showed interest — replied, booked,
downloaded, applied, answered a DM?"*

Not followers. Not impressions. Not list size. Not "reach." If the answer is a number
under 10, the diagnosis is volume and everything else in this session is downstream of
that.

**The full advertising sentence**, which the rest of this skill fills in:

```
Get MORE PEOPLE  to SEE something  that makes them ENGAGE  so they become LEADS
      ↓                  ↓                    ↓                        ↓
  Core Four        the creative        the lead magnet          the CTA
   (Step 2)         (Step 2)             (this step)            (this step)
```

## Second: is the offer worth advertising?

One question: *"Of the last 20 people who saw your offer properly, how many bought?"*

If the answer is zero and the sample is real, stop and send them to `/100m-offers`.
Advertising a broken offer just buys you a more expensive way to find out it's broken.
Say it in those words.

## The lead magnet

A lead magnet is a **complete solution to a narrow problem**, given free, that creates the
next problem — the one your paid offer solves. Complete matters: a half-solution that
leaves them stuck makes you look like the problem.

**The governing principle: give away the secret, sell the implementation.** Knowing what
to do has never been why people pay. They pay for speed, certainty, and not having to do
it themselves. When a user resists revealing their method, name that fear directly — the
people who would have done it themselves were never going to buy, and the people who
would buy are now convinced you know what you're doing.

### The six steps

Walk these in order, one question each.

**1. Pick a narrow problem you actually solve.**
Ask: *"What's the smallest problem your customer has that you could completely solve for
them in under an hour of their time?"* Narrow beats impressive. "How to price a
commercial roof" beats "the complete guide to running a roofing business."

**2. Pick the type.** Three that work, in rough order of how well they qualify:

| Type | What it is | Best when |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Reveal the problem** | Diagnose something they didn't know was wrong — an audit, scorecard, teardown, calculator | The buyer doesn't yet know they have the problem. Strongest for high-ticket B2B. |
| **Sample or trial** | A limited taste of the real thing — free session, first month, limited-feature version | The product is obviously good once experienced. |
| **One step of a multi-step process** | Solve step 1 completely; steps 2–7 are the paid offer | There's a genuine sequence and step 1 is real work. |

**3. Pick the delivery format.** Whatever is fastest to consume and fastest for the user
to produce. Checklists, templates, spreadsheets, short videos, live workshops, software,
physical items. Bias toward **tools over information** — a spreadsheet that does the
calculation beats an article explaining the calculation.

**4. Make it valuable enough that you could sell it.** The test: *"Would someone pay $100
for this?"* If not, it won't convert, because free things that look free get treated as
free. Anchor the price on the page: "normally $200."

**5. Make it easy to consume.** Under 20 minutes to value. If it takes a weekend, it
doesn't get used, and an unused lead magnet generates no engaged leads. Cut it down.

**6. Name it and attach the CTA.** Same MAGIC logic as an offer name — say who it's for,
what they get, and how fast. Then **the CTA is the whole point**: the lead magnet is not
the goal, the next step is. Every lead magnet ends with one specific, singular ask.

```
"The 10-Minute Roof Quote Calculator"
  → CTA: "Want us to price your next three jobs with you? Book a 15-min call."

"The HVAC Owner's Missed-Call Audit"
  → CTA: "Reply AUDIT and I'll record a 5-minute teardown of your intake."
```

### Getting it delivered

Ask: *"When someone asks for it, what happens in the next 60 seconds?"* The gap between
"raises hand" and "receives it" is where most lead magnets die. It does not need to be
automated on day one — sending it manually is completely acceptable and often better,
because the reply becomes the conversation.

### Checks before moving on

- Does it solve one problem **completely**? (Not partially. Partially is worse than
  nothing.)
- Does solving it **create the next problem** your paid offer solves?
- Could you **charge** for it?
- Is it consumable in **under 20 minutes**?
- Is there **exactly one** CTA?

If any answer is no, fix it here. A weak lead magnet makes every channel in Step 2 look
like it doesn't work.
