# Step 2: The Core Four — pick exactly one

There are only four ways to advertise. Everything else is a variation.

|                  | **Warm** (they know you) | **Cold** (they don't) |
|------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| **One-to-one**   | 1. Warm outreach          | 3. Cold outreach       |
| **One-to-many**  | 2. Post free content      | 4. Paid ads            |

Read the whole table to the user. Then make them pick one. **The single most common
failure in this entire skill is a user leaving with two channels.** They will do neither.

## How to choose

Ask three questions, then prescribe — don't offer a menu:

1. *"How many people already know you — customers, past customers, colleagues, anyone who
   would recognise your name?"*
2. *"Do you have money you can lose for 90 days without it mattering?"*
3. *"Would you rather spend an hour a day talking to individuals, or an hour a day
   making something?"*

The prescription:

| Situation | Channel | Why |
|-----------|---------|-----|
| Zero customers ever | **Warm outreach** | Non-negotiable. You don't yet know what message works; the only people who will tolerate you finding out are people who already know you. |
| Has a list of 100+ contacts, no sales | **Warm outreach** | The list is the asset. Working it costs nothing and produces the message you'll use everywhere else. |
| Some customers, no budget, likes making things | **Content** | Compounds, costs time not money, slowest to start. |
| Some customers, no budget, hates making things | **Cold outreach** | Fastest feedback, hardest daily grind, works from day one. |
| Working offer + budget + one organic channel already converting | **Paid ads** | Amplifies a known-good message. Never run ads to find the message. |

**The ads rule.** Do not let a user go to paid ads before something else converts. Ads
amplify — including amplifying a message that doesn't work, at cost. If they insist, agree
on a hard spend cap and a kill number before they start, and write both in the doc.

---

## 1. Warm outreach

Contacting people who already know you. Boring, unscalable, and the highest-converting
thing available to anyone starting out.

**Build the list.** Phone contacts, email contacts, every social platform's follower and
following lists, past clients, past colleagues, alumni, group chats. Ask them to actually
count it. It is almost always 500–2,000 people and they think it's 30.

**The message shape — ACA:**
- **A**cknowledge — something true and specific about them, not a template variable
- **C**ompliment — genuine, tied to the acknowledgement
- **A**sk — soft, singular, easy to say no to

```
"Hey Marcus — saw you took over the Northside branch, congrats.
 You were the only person at Kestrel who actually answered support tickets on time,
 so that tracks.

 Random one: I put together a 10-minute calculator that prices commercial roof jobs.
 Want me to send it over? No pitch, genuinely just want a couple of people to
 tear it apart."
```

**The rules:**
- Lead with the free thing, never the sale.
- One person at a time, actually personalised. The moment it reads as a blast, it stops
  being warm outreach and starts being spam from someone they know, which is worse.
- Ask for a referral after every no: *"Know anyone who'd find this useful?"*
- Volume target: 100 contacts a day is the stated bar; even 20 done properly beats a
  thousand copy-pasted.

## 2. Post free content

One-to-many, to people who chose to follow. Slowest to start, compounds hardest, and the
only channel that keeps working after you stop paying.

**Structure every piece as Hook → Retain → Reward:**

| Part | Job | How |
|------|-----|-----|
| **Hook** | Earn the first three seconds | Ask a question they'd answer yes to, make a claim they'd argue with, or start mid-story. The hook is most of the outcome. |
| **Retain** | Keep them past the middle | Cut everything that isn't the point. Change something — angle, tone, pace — every few beats. |
| **Reward** | Make it worth having watched | They should be able to *do* something differently. Teach the actual thing. |

**The rules:**
- **Give away your best material.** The instinct to hold back the good stuff is the reason
  most content doesn't work. Value given publicly is the entire mechanism.
- **Volume before quality.** The first fifty posts are how you learn what lands. Nobody's
  fiftieth is worse than their first, and nobody's first was good.
- **Ask for engagement explicitly**, once, at the end. Most people won't unless asked.
- **Post where your buyer already is**, not where you're comfortable.
- Repurpose one idea across formats rather than inventing new ideas daily.

**The realistic expectation to set out loud:** content typically takes 3–6 months to
produce meaningful lead flow. If the user needs customers this month, this is the wrong
channel and you should say so rather than let them discover it in March.

## 3. Cold outreach

Contacting strangers directly. Fastest feedback loop of the four; hardest to sustain.

**The sequence:**
1. **Build a real list.** Narrow and accurate beats large and vague. Ask what defines a
   good-fit account in observable terms — headcount, tools they use, a job posting, a
   recent event.
2. **Find a reason to contact *this* person.** Something on their site, a hire, a review,
   a post. This is the entire difference between outreach and spam.
3. **Lead with big, fast, free value.** Not "can I have 15 minutes." Something they can
   use whether or not they reply: an audit, a teardown, a specific observation.
4. **Make the ask tiny.** "Want me to send it?" converts many times better than "want to
   book a call?" Get a yes to something small first.
5. **Follow up.** Most replies come after the first message. Three to five touches across
   channels, then stop.

```
Subject: your intake form

Ran through the booking flow on kestrelhvac.com — it asks for a service address
before it asks for a phone number, so anyone who bails at step 2 is unreachable.

Recorded a 4-minute teardown with two other things I noticed. Want it?

— no pitch, and I'll go away if not
```

**The rules:**
- Volume is the whole game, and volume is what people quietly avoid. Get the daily number
  in writing.
- Personalise the first line, template the rest. Fully-manual doesn't scale;
  fully-templated doesn't work.
- Track: contacted → replied → booked → showed. If the user isn't tracking, that's the
  first assignment.
- Respect the law. Ask what jurisdiction their prospects are in and flag CAN-SPAM / GDPR /
  local rules as something they need to verify. Do not advise on the specifics of
  compliance — say clearly that it needs checking.

---

## 4. Paid ads

Buying attention from strangers at scale. The only channel that scales on money instead
of time, which is why everyone wants to start here and why almost nobody should.

**Prerequisites — all three, no exceptions:**
1. An offer that already converts, with a real close rate you can name.
2. Money you can lose entirely for 90 days.
3. Tracking that actually attributes a sale back to a click.

If any is missing, say so and put the user back on one of the other three.

**The creative structure — Callout → Value → CTA:**
- **Callout** — name the person in the first line. *"HVAC owners doing $1–3M:"* Everyone
  else scrolls, which is the point.
- **Value** — the dream outcome and why it's believable. Show the thing, don't describe it.
- **CTA** — one instruction, and it should ask for the lead magnet, not the sale.

**The rules:**
- **What you say matters more than where you say it.** Users obsess over platform choice
  and under-invest in the message. Push the effort toward the creative.
- Test creative, not settings. Creative is where the variance lives.
- Set a spend cap and a kill number before launch, and write both in the doc.
- Expect to lose money for a while. Ads that pay back immediately are the exception, which
  is exactly why the money model matters — see `/100m-money`.

**Never state specific current CPMs, CPCs, or platform costs from memory.** They change
constantly and are wildly industry-dependent. If a number is needed, tell the user to pull
it from their own account or run a small test to find it.
