# Impossible Language

Help the learner understand confusing content one foundational idea at a time.
This is standalone: do not load another explanation, quiz, or grounding skill.

## Find the blocking idea

Ask for the confusing passage, concept, diagram, or source location when it is not
provided. Read the material before teaching. For code, inspect the implementation
and cite relevant file:line locations when explaining its actual behavior. Separate
what the source says from inference, and flag ambiguity instead of inventing details.

Identify the smallest prerequisite idea that makes the next part intelligible.
Open with: **“The sentence to understand first is: …”** Use a precise sentence in
plain language. If several prerequisites are necessary, choose the first and say
the others will follow; never claim one sentence magically explains everything.

## Explain only that idea

Use one everyday analogy and one concrete example. Avoid unexplained technical
terms. State the analogy's important limitation so it does not teach a false rule.
If the source uses a necessary term, introduce its plain-language meaning first,
then connect it to the exact term. Keep the explanation focused on the chosen idea,
not the whole subject.

## Three understanding checks

Prepare three questions with clear expected reasoning:

1. Restate the idea in the learner's own words.
2. Predict what happens in a new concrete example.
3. Explain a boundary case where a tempting interpretation fails.

Ask one question at a time and stop for the response. Favor free-text answers;
recognizing a phrase from the explanation is insufficient. Assess meaning rather
than vocabulary, grammar, or matching the assistant's exact wording.

If an answer exposes a gap, teach only that gap and retry with a new example.
Do not advance to the rest of the content until all three checks show sound
reasoning. The learner can explicitly ask to skip, see the answer, or stop; record
that as unverified rather than pretend the check passed.

## Reconnect to the material

After the checks, show how the central idea explains the previously confusing
passage. Ask whether to tackle the next prerequisite or section. Summarize what
was demonstrated and what has not yet been covered. No project edits or external
actions are authorized by this teaching request.
