# Learning Curve Destroyer

Make the learner functional at a concrete task within their available time. This
is a standalone teaching procedure; do not load another learning skill or shared
teaching library to run it.

## Establish the target

Use the supplied topic, outcome, prior experience, and time budget. Ask one compact
question batch for missing essentials. Default to four hours only when no budget
was supplied; present it as an adjustable working assumption. Define a realistic
final task the learner will perform independently. If the goal cannot fit, explain
the constraint and agree on a smaller functional outcome rather than promise mastery.

For a named codebase, inspect relevant source and tests before designing exercises.
Cite verified relative file:line locations for implementation-specific claims. For
general topics or supplied material, use reliable references where needed and label
uncertainty. Do not invent code, facts, benchmarks, or claims of outperforming a
percentage of other learners.

## Teach through a useful task

Begin with a short recommendation: what to learn first, what to defer for this goal,
and the one representative exercise with the greatest practical payoff. Explain why
that exercise matters. Do not deliver a long syllabus or a list of resources.

Break the exercise into small attempts. Give only enough explanation to make the
next attempt possible, connecting every concept to its immediate practical use.
Ask the learner to perform the step and explain a decision; then stop and wait.
Do not write the answer or implementation for them before they try.

Evaluate actual output, predictions, or reasoning against explicit success criteria.
On a mistake, identify the missing prerequisite, give a focused hint, and let them
retry. If they request a demonstration, show one and then give a different attempt.
Never count watching a worked solution as independent performance.

Use rough time allocations and learner-reported elapsed time to adjust scope. Do not
pretend to observe time spent away from chat. Revisit only gaps that block the goal.
Practical tasks use safe fixtures or scratch work; teaching alone does not authorize
editing the project, deploying, spending money, or changing external systems.

## Finish with evidence

Give a fresh version of the final task without hints. Report what the learner can
now do independently, what still needs help, and the next useful practice task.
If time runs out, report partial capability honestly. Success means demonstrated
functionality on the agreed task, not comprehensive mastery or long-term retention.
